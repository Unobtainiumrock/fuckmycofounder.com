# Relationship Review safety — primary-source research

Researched: 2026-08-04
Scope: first-hand Reviews about identifiable, living adults on a US-facing
startup-professional network. This is product and technical-design research,
not legal advice or a conclusion that any law or safe harbor applies.

## Executive finding

A relationship selector and an attestation are necessary, but they do not make
a Review true, non-defamatory, private, or resistant to retaliation. The safer
launch contract is: an accountable Account; one first-hand relationship period;
a narrowly described relationship-verification state; prompts about observable
events rather than labels or diagnoses; pre-publication moderation; a single
moderated subject response; and a versioned report, notice, and appeal record.
Anonymous attribution must hide the Account from other users while warning that
the narrative, dates, and images may identify the author.

Do not present **Relationship verified**, an Exhibit, a rating, or a moderator's
approval as proof that the Review's allegations are true. Glassdoor expressly
separates account validation and integrity review from full identity,
employment, and truth verification; Rate My Professors likewise limits ratings
to first-hand relationships while stating that moderators cannot adjudicate the
underlying facts ([Glassdoor authenticity](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/trust/fighting-fake-reviews/),
[Rate My Professors guidelines](https://www.ratemyprofessors.com/guidelines)).

## 1. First-hand testimony and defamation/privacy boundaries

California's statutory definition of libel includes a false, unprivileged fixed
publication that tends to injure a person in their occupation. This is one
illustration of why a professional-person Review is high-impact even when the UI
calls it a story or opinion ([Cal. Civ. Code §45](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=45.)).
The product must not suggest that a score or phrases such as “I think” magically
insulate an implied factual claim. The exact state, audience, speaker, subject,
and statement matter; specialist counsel must set the final policy and copy.

Specify the following launch controls:

- The composer asks **what did you personally see or experience?**, **what
  happened next?**, and **what should a future collaborator know?** It rejects
  hearsay, statements made on another person's behalf, private contact details,
  protected-class commentary, medical or psychological diagnoses, confidential
  business records, and unverified accusations of criminal conduct.
- High and low assessments both require an optional-or-required concrete
  first-hand example according to the dimension's risk. **Not enough exposure**
  is always available. Ratings remain visibly scoped to this relationship.
- A moderator may publish, reject, or ask the author to revise; the platform
  does not silently rewrite the Review. Positive and negative Reviews receive
  the same integrity checks. The FTC's platform guidance recommends genuine-
  experience controls, equal treatment, no message-altering edits, and clear
  disclosure of collection, moderation, and display practices. Its precise
  legal application to Reviews of people is a counsel question, but it is the
  right integrity baseline ([FTC review-platform guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/featuring-online-customer-reviews-guide-platforms)).
- A Review is not the emergency, whistleblower, HR, or law-enforcement channel.
  When the draft describes imminent danger, criminal conduct, trade secrets, or
  protected reporting, interrupt publication with appropriate resources and a
  private safety route. Federal trade-secret law, for example, protects certain
  confidential disclosures to government or counsel—not publication to a
  general-purpose social network ([18 U.S.C. §1833](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&hl=false&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1833)).
- Remove exact home/personal contact data and information included to mobilize
  unwanted contact. California now provides a civil action for a defined form
  of doxing and defines identifying characteristics broadly; jurisdiction and
  intent remain legal questions, not moderation guesses
  ([Cal. Civ. Code §1708.89](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&lawCode=CIV&part=3.)).

Section 230's text distinguishes an interactive-computer-service provider from
an entity responsible, in whole or in part, for developing the information.
The app's mandatory prompts, labels, summaries, ranking, and Share Clips make
the exact boundary fact-specific. Do not put “protected by Section 230” in the
product or plan around immunity without counsel
([47 U.S.C. §230](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section230)).
California's anti-SLAPP statute is also jurisdiction- and claim-specific, not a
substitute for safe product design or litigation planning
([Cal. Civ. Proc. Code §425.16](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=425.16.)).

## 2. Relationship evidence without turning verification into a truth badge

Use three legible states, each with helper text:

1. **Self-attested relationship** — the Account selected a directional
   relationship, supplied an approximate date range and duration, and made the
   first-hand/material-accuracy attestation.
2. **Relationship evidence reviewed** — a trained moderator reviewed private,
   minimally sufficient evidence that the relationship likely existed. This
   does not validate the narrative or scores.
3. **Relationship disputed** — the subject or integrity system challenged the
   relationship and the Review is hidden from distribution while a different
   moderator rechecks it. A cleared dispute restores the previous state; a
   failed claim removes the Review and opens an appeal.

At launch, accept narrow evidence classes rather than “upload anything”: a
work-domain or institutional affiliation signal, or a redacted work artifact
showing both parties and the approximate period. Subject confirmation may be an
option for a named Review, but must not be solicited for an anonymous Review
because the request itself can identify the reviewer. Record the evidence
class, reviewer, decision, and expiry. Keep the artifact private, never show it
to the Profile subject, and delete it under the foundation's retention schedule.
Do not display a universal **verified reviewer** badge.

The public Relationship Claim should show the directional relationship and a
coarse period/duration. Company, project, exact dates, evidence, and verification
method are private by default because their combination can identify an
anonymous reviewer. An anonymous subject-dispute flow must ask the subject for
independent reasons the relationship is impossible; it must not reveal or
confirm guesses about the reviewer.

Enforce one active Review per Account, Profile subject, and relationship period.
Editing replaces the active version; it does not create a second contribution.
Account and device/risk signals may detect evasion, but a shared coworking IP or
office device is a signal, never automatic proof. Glassdoor combines account
validation, attestations, submission limits, algorithms, and human moderation
while acknowledging that it cannot fully verify every employment relationship
([Glassdoor authenticity](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/trust/fighting-fake-reviews/)).

## 3. Anonymous attribution is a system property

The composer must say plainly: **Anonymous means other users do not see your
Account. The platform knows who submitted this Review, and your details or
images may identify you. Valid legal process may require disclosure.** Glassdoor
warns that role, company, location, and other combined details can narrow a
speaker to a small group, and it does not promise anonymity against every court
order ([Glassdoor Terms](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/terms-2022-12-01/),
[Glassdoor legal requests](https://help.glassdoor.com/articles/en_US/Article/How-does-Glassdoor-respond-to-legal-requests-or-legal-action-to-find-out-who-posted-a-review)).

For an anonymous Review:

- publish no byline/Profile link, stable pseudonym, author activity, DM target,
  follower event, per-author analytics, or author identifier in URLs, exports,
  notifications, Share Clips, or client payloads;
- preflight exact dates, unique titles/projects, names, quoted private messages,
  and image text; offer coarser dates and redaction before submission;
- delay and bucket view/reaction analytics so timing cannot confirm the author;
- give the subject notice only after publication or a non-identifying delay,
  unless safety operations require otherwise; and
- restrict the Account linkage and raw evidence to trained trust-and-safety or
  legal roles, with a case reason and append-only access history.

Create a documented legal-request process: validate service and scope; preserve
only what a valid hold covers; notify the user when lawful and safe; allow time
to object; narrow or challenge overbroad demands where appropriate; and log the
decision. Courts use jurisdiction-specific standards to balance anonymous
speech and reputational claims, so the platform cannot promise the same result
in every case. Glassdoor's published process and cases are useful operating
precedent, not legal authority for this product
([Glassdoor anonymity cases](https://help.glassdoor.com/articles/en_US/Article/Tell-me-about-some-of-Glassdoor-s-successful-legal-efforts-to-defend-user-anonymity)).

## 4. Exhibits: moderated context, not evidence of truth

Launch with a small allowlist of static image types only. Re-encode every
accepted image into a platform-controlled derivative; use generated filenames;
validate decoded type and dimensions; enforce size/count limits; scan it; store
it outside the executable web path; and never serve the raw upload. Image
rewriting also removes extraneous payloads and metadata such as EXIF. OWASP
recommends allowlisting, actual content validation, generated filenames,
scanning, isolated storage, and image rewriting
([OWASP File Upload](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/File_Upload_Cheat_Sheet.html),
[OWASP Input Validation](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Input_Validation_Cheat_Sheet.html)).

Before moderation, show the reviewer the exact public derivative and require a
separate Exhibit attestation: they have the right to share it; it does not
contain confidential records, private contact data, intimate imagery, or
unredacted third-party identities; and it is presented in context. OCR and
automated detection can flag possible leaks but cannot replace this preview and
human review. Give moderators crop/blur/reject controls that create a new
version without overwriting the case snapshot.

Label public media **Exhibit supplied by reviewer**. If C2PA Content Credentials
exist, preserve the validation result as a narrow provenance signal; never turn
it into **verified**. The C2PA specification says provenance can show origin and
tamper history but cannot establish that content is true or factual, and absent
credentials do not imply falsehood
([C2PA explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html),
[C2PA harms model](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/security/Harms_Modelling.html)).

Provide image-specific reports for privacy/contact information, copyright,
manipulation/context, and intimate imagery. If the service seeks DMCA safe-harbor
protections, counsel must implement the designated-agent, notice,
counter-notice, repeat-infringer, and restoration procedures in 17 U.S.C. §512
([U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/onlinesp/)). As of May 19,
2026, the TAKE IT DOWN Act requires covered platforms to provide a conspicuous
request process and remove validly reported nonconsensual intimate depictions
and known identical copies within 48 hours. The safest Review policy is still to
prohibit all intimate imagery, while operating the statutory process in case
filters fail ([FTC compliance guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act)).

## 5. Anti-brigading, coercion, and retaliation

Treat a burst as a reason to reduce reach and investigate, not proof that every
Review is false. Before a suspicious Review or batch affects the Feed or Profile
aggregates, place it in an integrity hold. Evaluate shared Account, device,
network, timing, invitation, text-similarity, and relationship-graph signals;
require human review for removals. Reddit expressly prohibits multi-account and
coordinated voting, while Rate My Professors combines a one-relationship limit
with removal and temporary locks for dogpiling
([Reddit disruption policy](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043066412-Disrupting-Communities),
[Rate My Professors guidelines](https://www.ratemyprofessors.com/guidelines)).

Neutral Review requests may ask people with genuine relationships to contribute,
but must not preselect the rating, require proof of submission, reveal who
declined, reward a favorable outcome, or target only likely supporters. Never
sell Review removal or subject positive and negative Reviews to different
standards. Glassdoor prohibits coercion, incentives, proof requests, multiple
accounts, and ballot-box stuffing; the FTC warns against sentiment-conditioned
incentives and review suppression
([Glassdoor authenticity](https://www.glassdoor.com/about/trust/fighting-fake-reviews/),
[FTC Reviews and Testimonials Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).

Add **Report retaliation or coercion** to the Review and moderation case. It can
capture a threat, forced proof request, employment consequence, doxing attempt,
or coordinated pressure without exposing the reviewer to the subject. A credible
report should immediately stop direct contact and recommendation surfaces,
preserve the relevant records, and enter trained review. Do not promise that an
employee's post is legally protected: the NLRB says some work-related social
media is protected concerted activity, while individual griping and certain
misconduct may not be; status also varies by worker
([NLRB social-media guidance](https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/the-law/employees/social-media-0)).

## 6. Notice, response, appeals, and lifecycle

Model immutable Review versions and externally legible states: `draft`,
`submitted`, `under review`, `needs changes`, `published`, `integrity hold`,
`removed`, and `withdrawn`. A material edit returns to moderation and the old
public version stays unavailable while review is pending. Votes, Awards, Feed
reach, and Profile aggregation count only an eligible published version.

Every enforcement notice should identify the Review/version, policy clause,
action, effective time, appeal eligibility/deadline, and a non-identifying
summary of the reason. The reporter, reviewer, and claimed Profile subject get
only the notice needed for their role. Preserve the intake object, applicable
policy version, evidence snapshot, moderator decision, notices, and appeal as
an append-only case history.

A claimed Profile subject gets one named, relationship-scoped **Subject
response** attached to a published Review. It goes through the same moderation
rules; may clarify or disagree; may not identify, guess at, threaten, or contact
an anonymous author; and cannot edit the Review or its assessment. The reviewer
may report the response but does not receive a private reply channel. A subject
dispute and a subject response are separate: the first challenges eligibility
or policy compliance in private, while the second adds public context.

Appeals are available to the reviewer for rejection/removal and to the subject
for a denied relationship, privacy, impersonation, or policy report. A different
moderator should handle high-impact appeals where practical. A legal demand,
copyright notice, or TAKE IT DOWN request uses its specialist workflow and
deadline rather than the ordinary appeal queue.

Apple currently requires UGC apps to filter objectionable material, provide
reporting with timely responses, block abusive users, and publish contact
information; it warns that services used primarily for anonymous bullying or
objectification of real people do not belong on the App Store. Google Play also
requires robust ongoing moderation and in-app report/block functionality. These
are distribution gates, not optional polish
([Apple App Review Guidelines §§1.1–1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/),
[Google Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en)).

## Required specialist-counsel gates before launch

Counsel should approve the exact flows, policies, and operating procedures for:

1. defamation, privacy, publicity, harassment, doxing, and anti-SLAPP exposure
   across launch jurisdictions, including what the platform's prompts,
   summaries, ratings, ranking, and Share Clips “develop”;
2. anonymous-speaker subpoenas, notice/objection, preservation, emergency and
   law-enforcement requests, and disclosure access controls;
3. current/former worker speech, retaliation, confidentiality, trade secrets,
   nondisparagement clauses, protected reporting, and appropriate redirects;
4. copyright/DMCA operations, photo and screenshot rights, C2PA claims, and the
   now-effective TAKE IT DOWN Act process and 48-hour response capability;
5. retention and deletion of relationship evidence, raw Exhibits, moderation
   snapshots, anonymous linkage, risk signals, and legal holds; and
6. Terms, Community Guidelines, privacy/anonymity promise, attestation,
   verification labels, moderator training, insurance, and App Store/Play Store
   review materials.

Until these gates are closed, the spec may define the product contract and
required interfaces, but implementation must not claim legal compliance,
guaranteed anonymity, verified truth, or immunity.
