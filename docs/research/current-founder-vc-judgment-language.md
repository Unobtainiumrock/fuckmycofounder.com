# Current founder-judgment language

**Research date:** 2026-08-04
**Question:** What words do founders, VCs, accelerators, and startup operators
currently use to judge founder capability, and which of those words can survive
as public review labels?

## Conclusion

The current vocabulary is plainer and more compressed than the earlier
scorecard. The best product-language shortlist is:

1. **LARP** — performance versus substance; inverse/risk score.
2. **Agency** — makes progress without waiting for permission or perfect
   conditions.
3. **Technical** — can personally understand, build, debug, or make credible
   technical decisions in the company's actual domain.
4. **Taste** — consistently recognizes what is good, simple, and worth
   shipping when the answer is not obvious from data.
5. **Clock speed** — closes decision, communication, shipping, and learning
   loops quickly.
6. **Run it back** — would the reviewer voluntarily work with this person
   again?

These are terms people are using now; they do not sound like a branded
leadership framework. **Slope**, **judgment**, and **GTM** are credible reserve
terms, but each has a product problem described below.

The strongest first prototype is therefore:

> **LARP · Agency · Technical · Taste · Clock speed · Run it back**

This is a language recommendation, not evidence that these dimensions predict
startup outcomes. Reviews must still be relationship-scoped and allow **Not
enough exposure** for every dimension.

## Ranked evidence and product fit

### 1. LARP

**Current meaning:** Performing the identity and rituals of a founder while
producing little customer, product, revenue, or operating substance. The live
contrast in current posts is generally *looking like a founder* versus
*building, shipping, selling, or having real customers*.

**Evidence:**

- Founder Ish Baid described startup LARPing as work that looks important but
  drives no business value, contrasting networking and founder aesthetics with
  selling a product ([LinkedIn post, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ish-baid-11645b87_last-weekend-i-got-to-meet-my-hero-rob-snyder-activity-7303055448675344384-Qd9I)).
- A 2025 founder interview with Kit founder Nathan Barry used “LARPing as CEOs”
  and “playing startup vs. building a business” in exactly this sense
  ([Unf*ck My Startup, 2025-05-22](https://castbox.fm/episode/The-Surprising-Cost-of-Kit.com-with-Nathan-Barry-id6316682-id812595965)).
- The usage is current enough to support a polished parody product whose entire
  premise is circular fake revenue and “no product required, no customers
  required” ([LARP](https://www.larp.website/), accessed 2026-08-04).

**Product fit:** **Strong.** It is sharp, current, already accepted for the
product, and naturally invites receipts. Make it visibly inverse: a higher LARP
score is worse. Its anchors should describe an observed gap between claims and
work, not invite reviewers to diagnose motives.

### 2. Agency / high agency

**Current meaning:** Takes ownership, creates forward motion in ambiguity, and
does not wait to be assigned a fully specified task.

**Evidence:**

- YC's current hiring language defines high agency as taking ownership without
  waiting for direction, alongside moving quickly and evidence of real things
  built ([Y Combinator job page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/y-combinator/jobs/14lrMqm-community-builder-and-product-engineer), accessed 2026-08-04).
- A current W25 YC company says high agency means investigating ambiguity,
  developing opinions, and moving hard problems forward
  ([Mesh](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/mesh-2/jobs/TKKfvmn-founding-engineer), accessed 2026-08-04).
- Stedi founder Zack Kanter calls agency a top-level hiring criterion and frames
  it as treating outcomes as within one's control
  ([First Round podcast transcript](https://review.firstround.com/podcast/how-to-build-a-company-youll-run-forever-zack-kanter-founder-and-ceo-of-stedi/), accessed 2026-08-04).

**Product fit:** **Strong, but use `Agency`, not `High Agency Score`.** The term
is genuinely live but has become common enough to border on cliché. A concrete
prompt must ask whether the person moved problems forward without needing
permission, rescue, or perfect information.

### 3. Technical

**Current meaning:** The person can personally reason about and work on the
technology central to the company. In current AI discourse this includes
knowing what models and tools can actually do, making sound technical
tradeoffs, and being able to ship—not merely having an engineering credential.

**Evidence:**

- a16z says AI-native companies are largely led by technical founders who
  deeply understand how to work with LLMs; it distinguishes that fluency from
  the domain context still needed to build something useful
  ([David Haber, 2025-08-18](https://a16z.com/context-is-king/)).
- YC's own live role description pairs “strong coding ability,” shipped
  projects, technical ability, taste, and judgment rather than credentials
  ([Y Combinator job page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/y-combinator/jobs/14lrMqm-community-builder-and-product-engineer), accessed 2026-08-04).
- YC's cofounder-matching vocabulary gives the stricter historical definition:
  technical means a programmer, scientist, or engineer who can build the
  product without outside assistance
  ([YC, 2021-10-21](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/what-do-people-want-in-a-co-founder)).

**Product fit:** **Strong if the scope is explicit.** `Technical` is better than
`Technical Chops`, `Hacker`, or an invented metaphor. But the score must not
silently equate “founder” with “software engineer.” The prompt should evaluate
the technical depth required by that founder's role and company, with **Not
enough exposure** prominent.

### 4. Taste / product taste

**Current meaning:** Judgment about what is good, simple, coherent, and worth
shipping before exhaustive data exists. In the AI era it is increasingly used
to separate abundant production ability from the ability to choose and refine
the right output.

**Evidence:**

- A current W25 YC company explicitly asks for “taste for simplicity” and
  defines it as turning messy domains into elegant abstractions
  ([Mesh](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/mesh-2/jobs/TKKfvmn-founding-engineer), accessed 2026-08-04).
- YC's own current role description pairs technical ability with “strong taste
  and judgment” ([Y Combinator job page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/y-combinator/jobs/14lrMqm-community-builder-and-product-engineer), accessed 2026-08-04).
- Current startup roles use the bare word `Taste` for visual, writing, and brand
  judgment, and test it through shipping work rather than a resume
  ([Naive, founded 2025](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/naive/jobs/kRh5sWd-growth-engineer)).
- By late 2025, practitioners were explicitly discussing the “PM hype” around
  product taste, which is evidence both of adoption and of emerging cliché risk
  ([Supra Insider, 2025-12-08](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0yYnDLKwmKqC84gy1Aacrj)).

**Product fit:** **Strong.** Use `Taste` if the prompt spans product, brand, and
company choices; use `Product taste` only if it is deliberately product-only.
It needs behavioral anchors because reviewers otherwise substitute their own
aesthetic preferences.

### 5. Clock speed

**Current meaning:** How quickly someone processes information and closes
loops—not just hours worked. It includes decision latency, response latency,
shipping, feedback, and turning new information into action.

**Evidence:**

- A founder-turned-VC describes clock speed as speed in product cycles,
  communication, decisions, and feedback-to-action, based on founders he would
  back again ([David Stein, 2025-06-06](https://leaders.vc/blog/characteristics)).
- A current YC founder-authored role asks for “high agency and high clock
  speed,” defining it through driving the agenda, ruthless prioritization, and
  sharp communication
  ([Dataleap](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dataleap/jobs/3ROwnay-founding-deployment-strategist-munich-office), accessed 2026-08-04).
- Current First Round material distinguishes useful velocity from mere haste:
  it defines velocity as speed in the right direction
  ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/a-research-toolkit-for-the-discovery-phase/), accessed 2026-08-04).

**Product fit:** **Strong, with careful anchors.** `Clock speed` sounds more
current and less workshop-made than `Ship Score`. Do not reward recklessness,
performative overwork, or message responsiveness alone; score how quickly the
person closes consequential loops while preserving decision quality.

### 6. Run it back

**Current meaning:** The compressed retrospective verdict: knowing the actual
experience, would you voluntarily choose another working relationship with
this person?

**Evidence:**

- a16z's 2025 founding-team guide begins sourcing with people one would “work
  with again in a heartbeat”
  ([Zabie Elmgren, 2025-08-28](https://a16z.com/how-to-hire-a-strong-founding-team/)).
- The Leaders Fund founder-quality piece frames its list around the best
  founders the author would bet on again
  ([David Stein, 2025-06-06](https://leaders.vc/blog/characteristics)).

**Product fit:** **Excellent as the summary, not an input to an aggregate
score.** `Would you run it back?` is more natural and revealing than “overall
founder quality.” Preserve the relationship context.

## Credible reserve terms

### Slope

Current startup usage means trajectory or rate of growth and learning, not
present credentials. A current YC founder says he indexes on “energy, values,
and slope” ([Dataleap](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dataleap/jobs/3ROwnay-founding-deployment-strategist-munich-office)); a16z uses `high-slope`
for candidates with greater growth potential and says early hires define a
company's slope ([a16z, 2025-08-28](https://a16z.com/how-to-hire-a-strong-founding-team/)).

**Why it is not in the core:** It is current, compact, and non-cringe, but it
is mostly talent/recruiting language. A reviewer needs meaningful observation
over time to rate someone's slope. Good optional dimension for longer
relationships; bad mandatory score.

### Judgment

Current operator language uses `judgment`, often paired with taste, to mean
choosing sound tradeoffs under uncertainty. Mesh uses `systems judgment` for
balancing correctness, speed, maintainability, and scale
([Mesh](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/mesh-2/jobs/TKKfvmn-founding-engineer));
YC pairs taste and judgment in its own current role
([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/y-combinator/jobs/14lrMqm-community-builder-and-product-engineer)).

**Why it is not in the core:** It is real language but so broad that it absorbs
Taste, Technical, and Clock speed. It works better inside their behavioral
anchors than as a separate public score.

### GTM / commercially sharp

`GTM`, `founder-led sales`, and `commercially sharp` are all live. Bessemer's
2025 founder playbook says founders should land the first dozen customers
themselves
([Bessemer, 2025-02-10](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-founders-playbook-for-scaling-to-1-million-arr));
a current YC founder-authored role uses `commercially sharp`
([Dataleap](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dataleap/jobs/3ROwnay-founding-deployment-strategist-munich-office)).

**Why it is not in the core:** It is essential for many CEOs, but reviewers may
mistake personality or charisma for commercial ability, and the relevance
varies by founder role and company stage. If tested, prefer the unadorned label
`GTM` with anchors around customers, positioning, and closing—not `Founder
Gravity` or `Can sell ice to Eskimos`-style copy.

### Low ego / high ownership

The pairing is highly current: 2025–26 YC company roles repeatedly use `high
agency, low ego` and `low ego, high ownership`
([Contrario](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/contrario/jobs/ShQCYs6-talent-operator),
[Vector Legal](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/vector-legal/jobs/qNxMUbU-founding-gtm-manager),
[Dataleap](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dataleap/jobs/3ROwnay-founding-deployment-strategist-munich-office)).

**Why it is not in the core:** It is excellent interview and reference-check
language but a dangerous public rating. `Ego` invites amateur personality
diagnosis; `ownership` overlaps Agency. Use concrete collaboration prompts in
the written review instead.

## Terms to avoid or quarantine

| Term | Status | Why it should not be a score label |
|---|---|---|
| **Cracked** | Live meme; a 2025 YC company literally titles a role “Cracked Engineer” ([Casco](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/casco/jobs/QAWjeXH-founding-engineer)). | Binary superlative, generationally narrow, and likely to age fast. Useful in marketing copy at most. |
| **Killer** | Still appears in current startup recruiting copy ([Aios](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/aios/jobs/vMi2M3G-head-of-talent-at-aios-medical-remote-125k-250k-yr-inc-equity)). | Aggressive, exclusionary, and already dated; particularly poor beside safety/moderation copy. |
| **Hustle / grit** | Still in use—Index investor Mark Xu says “Hustle, empathy, obsession” and “ship the fastest” ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/team/mark-xu/)). | Generic and moralizes work intensity; hard to distinguish from hours, precarity, or performance. |
| **Relentlessly resourceful** | Canonical historical YC language from 2009 ([Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/relres.html)). | Semantically strong but stale as a *current* UI label and too long. Agency now carries much of the same meaning. |
| **Founder mode** | Major 2024 discourse term ([Paul Graham, 2024-09](https://paulgraham.com/foundermode.html)). | A management doctrine for scaling companies, not an individual capability; easily weaponized to excuse micromanagement. |
| **Founder-market fit** | Familiar VC term, but not a stable property of a person. | It evaluates a founder-market pairing, not how someone behaved in a past relationship. Bessemer has called it a retroactive observation of little substance ([Adam Fisher, 2022-09-19](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/a-brainstorming-framework-for-aspiring-founders)). |
| **10x** | Familiar tech shorthand, not a fresh founder term. | Unmeasurable mythology; reviewers cannot reliably normalize it across roles, stages, and resources. |
| **Founder gravity / cockroach / raccoon / ship score** | Product-invented language, not evidenced current judgment vocabulary. | Sounds like a naming workshop. It makes reviewers learn the product's metaphors before they can answer. |

## Product implications

- Use the short noun as the visible label: **Agency**, not “Agency Score” or
  “High-Agency Operator Index.”
- Put the personality in the anchor copy and answer choices, not in invented
  metric names.
- Ask about observed behavior in the stated relationship. None of these is a
  claim about a person's permanent character or objective odds of success.
- Keep **Not enough exposure** on Technical, Taste, Clock speed, and any future
  GTM or Slope dimension.
- Do not sum the dimensions into one Founder Score. The useful artifact is a
  relationship-scoped shape plus **Run it back**.
- Before freezing the scorecard, test the six labels in five-minute interviews
  with current founders and operators. Ask each participant to define the label
  before showing the product's definition; mismatches reveal jargon that is not
  actually shared.

## Source-quality note

This scan prioritized current, first-party discourse: founder-authored job
pages, accelerator materials, VC writing, and practitioner transcripts. Live
job pages are unusually useful for vocabulary because they expose the words
operators currently use when real selection is at stake. They do not establish
predictive validity. The social posts and podcast descriptions cited for
`LARP` are cultural evidence only, not authority about founder performance.
