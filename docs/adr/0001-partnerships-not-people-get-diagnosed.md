# Subjects must consent; partnerships, not people, get diagnosed

Every feature that could describe a human — the Cooked Quiz, archetypes, the
leaderboard, future profiles and references — follows one rule: authors may be
anonymous, but a person only appears as a subject (face, name-as-profile,
ranking, diagnosis) if they opted in. Where a feature wants a human-shaped
subject without consent, it gets a partnership ("How cooked are *we*?") or a
fictional archetype (The Ghost) instead.

## Considered Options

The rejected alternative — diagnosing/ranking the absent cofounder ("Is your
cofounder cooked?", real-person leaderboards, rate-anyone reviews) — is more
viral and will be re-proposed regularly. It was rejected deliberately: it
converts the product from confession to accusation, which is the documented
failure mode of its comparables (Tea's lawsuits and breach fallout, Peeple's
collapse), poisons the brand's "catharsis, not harassment" position, and
creates moderation/defamation liability we have no infrastructure to carry.

## Consequences

Quiz results never name or type a person. Archetypes are illustrated fictional
characters only. Leaderboards rank stories and archetypes, never individuals.
Contact-info blocking in validation is load-bearing, not vestigial. Profiles
and references (Phase 4) require explicit opt-in and verification.
